From the Editor

This is a working almanac — part résumé, part field journal — kept by Sujay Mahadik, an SRE-by-day at Barclays in Knutsford and a designer-by-disposition since 2016¹. Most days are spent shaping data: from IBM Netcool into Kafka, from raw events into legible dashboards, from noise into signal.

Other days are spent on the inverse: turning signal into noise, on purpose² — type set on a grid, posters for fandoms nobody asked for, an album cover, a photograph of a quiet street.

“Build the pipeline, then write the poem about the pipeline.”

Pan right ⟶ for the index, the photo plates, the now-page, and a small collection of essays on green software and the ethics of dashboards.

About the Author
An economist by degree, an engineer by trade, a designer by temperament

The Long Version

I write data pipelines for a living and pretend I'm setting type for a periodical. The two crafts are closer than they look — both are about deciding what counts as a unit, where the gutters go, and which detail rewards a closer look.

I grew up in Pune, studied Information Technology at MIT College of Engineering, then doubled back for a Master's in Economics at Fergusson³ because I wanted a vocabulary for the things engineering kept eliding.

Since 2019 I've been at Barclays — first as a Graduate Analyst, then on Service Delivery for Enterprise Data Integration, and now on the SRE Core team in Knutsford, building observability for Infrastructure. Translation: I make it so the people running banks at 3 a.m. can actually see what's going on.

On the side: Infin8y Labs, a fandom-poster studio with a friend; The Jellyfish Collective, a small branding outfit; photography; the occasional essay on green software and the ethics of measurement.

I keep a Now page because résumés are bad at saying what someone is like. The currency of a portfolio, I think, is the temperature of the room.
